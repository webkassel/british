What we want to answer

From chartered companies to direct rule from London.

Reasons · 01 Trade

The Empire began as a business

  • British involvement in triangular trade started with the colonization of America in 1607 and the West Indies in 1623.
  • Two chartered companies laid the ground: the East India Company (founded 1600, under Elizabeth I) and the Virginia Company (under James I), which created the first North American colony — the Jamestown Colony of 1607.
  • The book calls these the foundations of the growth and rise of the British Empire.
  • Many colonies began as trading centres or were founded to protect a trade route — and they were run for the profit of the mother country.
  • The chief British ports were London, Liverpool, Bristol and Glasgow.
Reasons · The engine

One triangle, three profits

  • Triangular trade means trade among three ports or regions. The best-known system is the transatlantic trade, running from the seventeenth to the early nineteenth century.
  • European goods were used to buy slaves from traders in Africa or the Caribbean.
  • Slaves were shipped to the Americas on the sea lane called the middle passage — a horrible journey on which many died of disease and maltreatment.
  • The crops they were forced to grow — cotton, tobacco, sugar — were then exported to Europe.

Reasons · 03 The cost

Built on forced labour

  • 1501 — the slave trade is started by Portuguese and Spanish traders.
  • 1807 — the UK Parliament passes a bill that officially abolishes the trading of slaves.
  • Illegal slave trade across the Atlantic continued until the second half of the nineteenth century.
  • There are an estimated 27 million victims of slavery worldwide today.
Justification

A duty they gave themselves

  • Some people believed it was a moral obligation and a destiny to govern poorer, supposedly less advanced countries.
  • Their stated aim was to pass on European culture to the native inhabitants.
  • Rudyard Kipling called this the 'white man's burden'.
  • Around 1900 Britain was one of the greatest economic and political powers in the world — which made the claim to lead others easy to believe at home.

Development · How it was run

Trading stations become the Raj

London

From 1801 the Colonial Office managed the empire. District officers and civil servants were sent out to administer the colonies on Britain's behalf, and regular imperial conferences in Britain settled questions of trade, defence and foreign policy.

India

Controlled for years by the East India Company, which built roads and railroads to make trade easier. A Governor-General was put in charge, backed by British troops. After 1858 a viceroy replaced him and influence grew into the Raj — British rule.

The jewel in the crown

India brought Britain great wealth and strategic advantage. Local Indian rulers were allowed to remain in power as long as they stayed loyal to the viceroy. Many British families spent years in India as civil servants, engineers and police officers.

Development · The return journey

The Empire changed Britain too

  • Britain did not only import foreign goods — there was also a great influence of foreign ideas, especially from India.
  • In the eighteenth century curry recipes and the famous 'mulligatawny soup' — Tamil for 'pepper-water' — appeared in Britain.
  • Indian designs influenced British art and architecture: the Royal Pavilion in Brighton was rebuilt in a 'Hindoo' style in the early nineteenth century.
  • Polo, snooker and billiards, played by British soldiers in India, were 'exported' back to Britain.
Conclusion

Three sentences to remember

  • Reason. The Empire rose out of trade and profit — colonies were founded as trading centres and run for the profit of the mother country.
  • Justification. It was presented as a moral obligation to govern and civilise others — Kipling's 'white man's burden' — while it was in fact paid for by sugar, cotton and forced labour.
  • Development. Within three centuries a handful of chartered companies grew into direct rule over a quarter of the world's land and population.
